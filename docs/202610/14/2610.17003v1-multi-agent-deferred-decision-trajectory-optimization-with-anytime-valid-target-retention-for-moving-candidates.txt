# Multi-Agent Deferred-Decision Trajectory Optimization with Anytime-Valid Target Retention for Moving Candidates 

Farbod Siahkali 1, Mengxue Hou 2, and Vijay Gupta 1

Abstract — We study multi-agent terminal interception when one of several moving candidates is the unknown target and both target identity and candidate terminal locations are uncertain. We develop a receding-horizon deferred-decision trajectory opti-mization (DDTO) controller that combines conformal terminal-location prediction, sequential candidate rejection, capacity-constrained assignment, and candidate-contingent planning. Un-der exchangeability conditions for terminal and identity residuals, with predictable querying, we establish fixed-time terminal-location coverage and anytime-valid target retention. At the final replan, when one control step remains, we bound the joint probability that a tightened-set DDTO plan passes nonlinear rollout verification but capture fails. The closed-loop controller applies fallback guidance to any agent without a verified DDTO plan and, across 100 paired scenarios, captures 79% of targets under the strict control-effort budget and 85% when that budget is relaxed. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Multi-robot autonomy naturally couples sensing and control, since motion affects what information can be collected and sensing, in turn, affects subsequent decisions [1]–[4]. We con-sider a team of agents tasked with intercepting one unknown target among several moving candidates, where this coupling arises through both target identification and motion planning. Neither the target identity nor the candidates’ terminal loca-tions are known when motion begins. The agents must therefore make progress toward inter-ception while preserving alternatives that may later become necessary. Committing too early can direct an agent toward a non-target, whereas excessive deferral can leave insufficient time or control authority for capture. The central difficulty is that inference, assignment, and trajectory planning are coupled [5]. Because target identity is unresolved when the current control is applied, each agent must choose a single input that preserves feasible trajectories toward all assigned candidates. Contingency and scenario-based planners maintain plans for multiple future realizations [6]–[8]. Contingency model predictive control (CMPC) shares the currently applied input across nominal and contingency plans [9], while delayed-decision motion planning (DDMP) preserves multiple feasible responses up to a prescribed delayed decision time [10].

DDTO constructs candidate-contingent branches with a com-mon prefix and delays their separation while preserving ter-minal feasibility [11], [12]. Existing DDTO formulations typically assume fixed can-didate terminal locations. Prior active-sensing DDTO [13], which incorporates sensing into the optimization, still consid-ers one agent, fixed terminal points, and candidate-set guaran-tees assessed at individual times. It leaves terminal-location uncertainty for moving candidates, anytime-valid target re-tention under adaptive querying, and multi-agent assignment unresolved. We consider these issues jointly in a receding-horizon multi-agent setting. Statistical and learning-based methods have been developed across both estimation and prediction settings [14]–[16]. Con-formal prediction (CP) provides model-agnostic uncertainty quantification for regression and classification problems [17], [18]. The two uncertainties considered here require different statistical guarantees. For terminal-location uncertainty, CP can provide marginal coverage of a candidate’s terminal lo-cation at any prespecified replanning index [19]–[22]. Identity decisions, however, are made repeatedly: at each replan, agents select which candidates to query using cur-rent tracking information and controller-visible history, before observing the corresponding identity measurements. Rejected candidates are not reconsidered. Consequently, applying a fixed-time test independently at each replan does not con-trol the probability of ever rejecting the target. The identity procedure must instead provide an anytime-valid retention guarantee under predictable querying, meaning that each query is selected from information available before the queried identity measurement is observed. We combine these components in a closed-loop multi-agent DDTO framework. At each replan, conformal termi-nal regions represent candidate terminal-location uncertainty. Candidate-wise online conformal p-values [23] are converted to e-values and accumulated in an e-process [24]. Threshold crossings then generate nested retained sets. With the target’s adjusted identity residuals conditionally exchangeable given the exogenous scene and other-candidate information, and with predictable querying, Ville’s inequality bounds the probability that the target is ever rejected [25]. The retained candidates are assigned subject to per-agent capacity, and each agent solves a DDTO problem whose branches share the applied control. At the final replan, when one control step remains, the controller uses a tightened terminal set. Target retention and terminal-location coverage then bound the probability that a verified DDTO plan is returned at the final replan but capture fails. B. Compared Controllers 

The CMPC [9] and DDMP [10] baselines use the same sensing, identity update, terminal regions, assignment, dynam-ics, fallback, effort constraints, and replanning procedure as Proposed DDTO. CMPC couples only the first control across branches, whereas DDMP fixes a three-stage common prefix. Proposed DDTO instead minimizes the branch-separation ob-jective in (8). The clustered-assignment variant sorts retained candidates by the first coordinate of their predicted terminal centers and partitions the ordered list into contiguous capacity-feasible assigned sets. Immediate–commit selects, for each agent, the candidate in its assigned set with the smallest W jk+1 , retains that choice until the candidate is rejected, and solves asingleton terminal trajectory problem at every replan. This gives a simpler committed single-trajectory baseline. Center– commit plans toward the centroid of the assigned candidates’ predicted terminal centers until ⌈0.95 T ⌉, after which it selects the candidate with the smallest W jk+1 .Fallback-only uses the same sensing, candidate rejection, reach-cost assignment, and fallback guidance policy as Pro-posed DDTO, but applies fallback guidance at every replan without solving a DDTO problem. Fallback-disabled retains DDTO planning but applies zero body force whenever no verified DDTO plan is available.

We presented a multi-agent DDTO framework for inter-ception under uncertain target identity and terminal location. The analysis establishes fixed-time terminal-location coverage, anytime-valid target retention, and a bound on the probability that a verified tightened-set plan is returned at the final replan but capture fails. Proposed DDTO achieves the highest observed capture rates among the planners: 79% and 85% 

under strict and relaxed effort budgets, respectively. Fallback ablations show that neither Fallback-disabled nor Fallback-only reproduces the performance of the complete controller.
